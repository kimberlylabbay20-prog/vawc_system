Know Your Rights

Learn about VAWC laws, your rights as a woman, and where to find help and protection.

What is VAWC?

Violence Against Women and Children (VAWC) refers to any act of gender-based violence that results in physical, sexual, psychological, or economic harm or suffering to women and children. This includes threats, coercion, harassment, and arbitrary deprivation of liberty — whether in public or in private life.

You are not alone. Help is available, and you have the right to live free from violence and fear. There are laws that protect you, people who will listen, and institutions ready to help.

Your Rights as a Woman

Right to Protection
Every woman has the right to be protected from all forms of violence and discrimination. The State is duty-bound to ensure your safety and well-being.
Right to Report Abuse
You have the right to report any incident of abuse to your barangay, the PNP, or DSWD without fear of retaliation or judgment.
Right to Legal Assistance
Free legal assistance is available through the Public Attorney's Office (PAO) and accredited non-government organizations.
Right to Medical Support
You are entitled to immediate medical treatment, examination, and counseling at any government hospital or health center.
Right to Privacy & Confidentiality
Your identity and the details of your case shall be kept confidential throughout the legal and investigation process.
Right to Shelter & Safety
Temporary shelter, protection, and psychosocial support are available through DSWD, local government units, and women's shelters.

Philippine VAWC Laws

RA 9262
Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act of 2004
Protects women and their children from physical, sexual, psychological, and economic abuse. Provides for Barangay Protection Orders (BPO), Temporary Protection Orders (TPO), and Permanent Protection Orders (PPO).

RA 9262 criminalizes acts of violence committed by a husband, partner, or any person with whom the woman has or had a marital, dating, or sexual relationship. Penalties range from arresto mayor to reclusion perpetua depending on the severity of the offense. Victims may apply for protection orders at the barangay or court level at no cost.

RA 8353
The Anti-Rape Law of 1997
Reclassifies rape as a crime against persons. Defines and penalizes rape and sexual assault, including marital rape. Provides protective measures for survivors during legal proceedings.

RA 8353 expanded the definition of rape to include both men and women as possible victims, and recognized marital rape as a criminal offense. The law also allows for in-camera proceedings to protect the privacy of the survivor and prohibits the publication of the survivor's identity without consent.

RA 7877
Anti-Sexual Harassment Act of 1995
Prohibits sexual harassment in employment, education, and training environments. Requires employers and institutions to create and implement policies against sexual harassment.

RA 7877 defines sexual harassment as any unwelcome sexual advance, request for sexual favor, or other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature made by a person who has authority, influence, or moral ascendancy over another in a work or educational setting. Employers who fail to take action may be held liable.

RA 11313
Safe Spaces Act (Bawal Bastos Law)
Prohibits gender-based sexual harassment in public spaces, online, and in workplaces. Covers catcalling, unwanted remarks, stalking, and all forms of public harassment.

RA 11313 or the Safe Spaces Act expands the scope of prohibited acts to include gender-based harassment committed in streets, public transportation, parks, online platforms, and workplaces. It penalizes misogynistic, homophobic, and transphobic remarks. Barangay officials are required to disseminate information and assist victims.
